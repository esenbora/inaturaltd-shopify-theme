Mineral or chemical: what is the difference?

Sunscreens work in one of two ways. Chemical filters absorb into the skin and turn UV into heat. Mineral (also called physical) filters, mainly zinc oxide and titanium dioxide, sit on top of the skin and reflect UV away. For sensitive skin, children and faces that react easily, a lot of people prefer mineral because it works on contact and tends to be gentler. We go deeper into the trade-offs in our guide to choosing sun protection for sensitive skin and kids.

What does SPF50 and broad-spectrum actually mean?

SPF measures protection against UVB, the rays behind sunburn. SPF50 filters out around 98% of UVB when applied properly, which is the catch: most people use far too little. Broad-spectrum means it also covers UVA, the deeper rays linked to premature ageing. Every INCIA sunscreen below is SPF50 and broad-spectrum. The number on the bottle only holds up if you apply a generous layer and top it up, so treat SPF as one part of the routine, not the whole thing.

How do you apply sunscreen properly?

Most of the protection people miss comes down to how they use it, not which one they buy. A few habits that make the biggest difference:

  • Apply a generous layer fifteen minutes before you head out.
  • Reapply every two hours, and always after swimming, sweating or towelling off.
  • Do not forget ears, the back of the neck, tops of feet and the parting in your hair.
  • Do not rely on SPF alone: a hat, sunglasses and shade between 11am and 3pm do a lot of the work.
