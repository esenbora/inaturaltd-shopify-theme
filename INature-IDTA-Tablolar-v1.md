İşbu belge, Taraflar arasında imzalandığında **geçerli ve bağlayıcı bir IDTA'dır.** ICO'nun kendi form dosyasına ayrıca aktarılması gerekmez.

ICO, Approved IDTA rehberinde bunu açıkça belirtir:

> "You don't need to use the specific format of the tables in Parts 1, 2 and 3, so long as you still set out the information required by those parts in your contract documentation."

Zorunlu olan iki şey vardır ve ikisi de bu belgede karşılanmıştır:

1. **Part 1 Tabloları eksiksiz doldurulmuş olmalıdır.** Taraflar, aktarımın detayları, aktarılan veri ve güvenlik gereklilikleri aşağıda Table 1 ile Table 4'te yer almaktadır. (Part 2 ve Part 3 ICO'ya göre isteğe bağlıdır; bu belgede yine de doldurulmuştur.)
2. **Part 4 Mandatory Clauses IDTA'ya dahil edilmiş olmalıdır.** ICO, bunun tam metni yazmak yerine atıfla yapılmasına izin verir ve bu hâlde kullanılacak "Alternative Part 4 Mandatory Clauses" metnini birebir belirler. O metin aşağıda Part 4'te aynen yer almaktadır.

Bu iki şart sağlandığı için Taraflar bu belgeyi doğrudan imzalar. ICO'nun yayımladığı tam metin (IDTA A.1.0, `ico.org.uk`) referans olarak saklanır; Part 4 hukuken o metne atıfla bu sözleşmenin parçası hâline gelir.

> **Dil.** Part 1, 2 ve 3'teki hukuki içerik, ICO terminolojisiyle birebir örtüşmesi için **İngilizce** düzenlenmiştir. Yanlarındaki Türkçe açıklamalar yalnızca okuma kolaylığı içindir ve hüküm doğurmaz. Çelişki hâlinde İngilizce metin esastır.

> **Madde numaraları.** Aşağıda Mandatory Clauses'a yapılan atıflar (örneğin "Section 16.1", "Section 29") ICO'nun A.1.0 metnindeki numaralandırmaya karşılık gelir. ICO metni ileride revize ederse, atıfta **hükmün adı** esas alınır, numarası değil.

# TABLE 3: TRANSFERRED DATA

## Transferred Data

☑ The personal data to be sent to the Importer under this IDTA consists of:

**Categories of Transferred Data:**

1. **Email addresses** of individuals who started but did not complete a purchase on the Exporter's online store ("abandoned checkouts").

2. Associated non-identifying transaction metadata for those abandoned checkouts, namely: date and time of the checkout, checkout value, and the titles of the products left in the basket.

**Express exclusions.** The Importer does not receive and does not process:

- names, postal addresses, telephone numbers or payment card data of any data subject;
- the customer email address recorded against a **completed** order (the Importer reads only non-identifying fields from order records, namely date, total value, sales channel, landing page, referring site and line item titles);
- any individual-level analytics record (Google Analytics 4 and Search Console data is accessed only in aggregated form);
- any personal data in connection with the blog content automation, which processes editorial content only.

> **Türkçe:** Aktarılan tek doğrudan tanımlayıcı **terk edilmiş sepetlerdeki e-posta adresidir.** Sipariş kayıtlarından kişisel veri okunmamaktadır. Bu, yönetim panelinin kaynak kodunda doğrulanmıştır.

☑ The Transferred Data has been reviewed and the Parties confirm it is limited to what is necessary for the Purpose.

## Special Categories of Personal Data and criminal convictions and offences

☑ The Transferred Data **does not include** any Special Category Data or criminal convictions and offences data.

> **Türkçe:** UK GDPR madde 9 ve 10 kapsamında hiçbir veri aktarılmamaktadır.

## Relevant Data Subjects

☑ The Data Subjects of the Transferred Data are:

Consumers located in the United Kingdom who began a checkout on the Exporter's online store and did not complete it.

**Approximate volume:** a maximum of 15 abandoned checkout records are retrieved and displayed at any one time; the Importer's interface is limited to the 15 most recent records by design. The cumulative number of data subjects over the term may be higher and is not recorded by the Importer.

## Purpose

☑ The Importer may Process the Transferred Data for the following purposes:

1. To display the Exporter's abandoned checkout data back to the Exporter within an administration panel built and operated for the Exporter, so that the Exporter can see which baskets are recoverable and which have a contactable email address.

2. To maintain, troubleshoot and keep operational the systems that provide that display.

☑ The Importer may Process the Transferred Data for these purposes **only**, and may not Process it for any purpose of its own.

**Express restrictions.** The Importer must not:

- sell, rent, license or otherwise commercially exploit the Transferred Data;
- use the Transferred Data for its own marketing or advertising;
- use the Transferred Data to train, fine-tune, evaluate or otherwise develop any machine learning or artificial intelligence model;
- carry out profiling or automated decision-making on the Transferred Data;
- enrich, append to or combine the Transferred Data with any other dataset;
- send any marketing communication to the Data Subjects without the Exporter's prior written instruction and confirmation of a lawful basis.

# TABLE 4: SECURITY REQUIREMENTS

## Security of Transmission

All Transferred Data is transmitted over **HTTPS/TLS** at every hop: between the Data Subject's origin platform (Shopify) and the Importer's systems, between the Importer's systems and the Exporter's browser, and between the Importer's systems and any third-party API.

No Transferred Data is transmitted over email, messaging applications, or any unencrypted channel.

## Security of Storage

**The Importer does not store the Transferred Data.**

The administration panel operated by the Importer contains no database, object store or persistent cache. Transferred Data is read from the Exporter's Shopify account on each request, held only in server memory for the duration of that request, rendered to the Exporter's browser, and discarded. It is never written to disk, exported, cached or backed up.

This has been verified against the application's dependency manifest, which contains no database, ORM or persistence component.

The only durable record of the Transferred Data remains within the Exporter's own Shopify account.

Because there is no storage, encryption at rest is not applicable to the Importer's processing.

## Security of Processing

- Access to the administration panel is protected by a shared password held only as an encrypted environment variable and never committed to source code.
- Password verification uses **constant-time comparison over SHA-256 digests**, mitigating timing attacks.
- Session state is carried in a cookie signed with **HMAC-SHA256**. Unsigned or incorrectly signed cookies are rejected.
- The session cookie is set `httpOnly` and `sameSite=lax`, and carries the `secure` flag in production so it is transmitted over HTTPS only.
- Sessions expire after **7 days**.
- If the password or signing secret is not configured, the system **fails closed**. Authentication cannot be bypassed.
- Access tokens for the Exporter's platforms are obtained through the `client_credentials` flow and rotated automatically. No long-lived static token is used.

## Organisational security measures

- Access to the Transferred Data is limited to personnel who need it to deliver the service.
- All personnel with access are bound by written confidentiality obligations that survive the end of the Linked Agreement.
- All code changes are held under version control and are attributable and reversible.
- Real customer data is not used in development or testing. The systems are designed to operate against placeholder records when no live data is bound.

## Technical security minimum requirements

- Data minimisation is enforced at the point of collection: the Importer's code requests only the specific non-identifying fields it needs from order records, and does not request name, address, telephone or payment fields at any point.
- Platform API permissions are scoped as narrowly as the platform allows.
- Dependencies are kept current.
- Hosting is on a managed platform providing infrastructure-level redundancy and monitoring.

## Updates to the Security Requirements

The Security Requirements may be updated by written agreement of the Parties, and must be reviewed on each Review Date set out in Table 2. No update may reduce the level of protection below that described above.

# PART 2: EXTRA PROTECTION CLAUSES

> Bu bölüm, TRA'da tespit edilen risklere karşı alınan ilave tedbirleri içerir. TRA ile birlikte okunmalıdır.

## 1. Extra technical security protections

1.1 The Importer confirms that it holds no persistent copy of the Transferred Data, as described in Table 4 (Security of Storage). The Importer must notify the Exporter in writing **before** introducing any database, persistent cache, export function, backup or other durable store that would retain the Transferred Data, and must not do so without the Exporter's prior written agreement.

1.2 The Importer must maintain the data minimisation described in Table 3, and in particular must not extend the fields it reads from the Exporter's order records to include any direct identifier, without the Exporter's prior written agreement.

1.3 The Importer must ensure that any access to the Transferred Data from outside the United Kingdom remains subject to this IDTA and to safeguards equivalent to those set out in Table 4 and in this Part 2.

## 2. Extra organisational protections

2.1 The Importer must keep access to the Transferred Data limited to personnel who need it to deliver the service.

2.2 The Importer must be able to demonstrate, on the Exporter's reasonable request, that the protections in this Part 2 remain in place.

## 3. Government and public authority access requests

3.1 If the Importer receives a request from any public authority, law enforcement body or court for access to the Transferred Data, the Importer must:

(a) notify the Exporter immediately, and in any event within **24 hours**, unless legally prohibited from doing so;

(b) where notification is legally prohibited, use all reasonable lawful efforts to obtain a waiver of that prohibition, and document those efforts;

(c) review the lawfulness and scope of the request, and challenge it where there is a reasonable basis to do so, including by seeking interim measures to suspend disclosure pending the outcome;

(d) disclose only the **minimum** amount of data that is strictly necessary to comply with a legally binding request;

(e) keep a written record of the request, the response and the steps taken, and make that record available to the Exporter on request.

3.2 The Importer must not voluntarily disclose the Transferred Data to any public authority in the absence of a legally binding request.

3.3 The Importer confirms that, as at the Start Date, it has received **no** such request in relation to the Transferred Data.

3.4 The Importer must notify the Exporter as soon as it becomes aware that it can no longer comply with this IDTA, including where a change in applicable law would prevent compliance. On such notification the Exporter may suspend the transfer and, if the position is not remedied, end the IDTA.

## 4. Transparency

4.1 On the Exporter's reasonable request, the Importer must provide the information necessary for the Exporter to demonstrate compliance with UK GDPR Chapter V, including confirmation of the hosting region and the current sub-processor list.

## 5. Practical mitigation arising from the Transfer Risk Assessment

5.1 The Parties record that the principal mitigation of the risk assessed in the Transfer Risk Assessment is structural rather than contractual: because the Importer retains no copy of the Transferred Data, there is no dataset at rest in the destination country capable of being obtained by a public authority, and any compelled disclosure would be limited to data transiently held in memory at the moment of the request.

5.2 The Parties record that the Transferred Data consists of email addresses and non-identifying transaction metadata only, contains no Special Category Data, and that a maximum of 15 records are accessible at any one time. The Parties consider that this, together with the absence of any retained dataset in the destination country, materially limits the severity of any harm arising from unauthorised access.
