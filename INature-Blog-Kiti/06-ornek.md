Winter in the UK is hard on small hands. The heating goes on in October, the air indoors dries out, and hands get washed far more often than in summer. By February a lot of children have red knuckles that crack open and sting in the bath.

It is not a serious problem, but it is an uncomfortable one, and it tends to keep coming back until something in the routine changes.

## Why it happens more to children

Children's skin is thinner than adult skin and holds on to moisture less well. They also wash their hands far more than they used to, often with whatever soap is in the school toilets, and often without drying them properly afterwards.

Wet hands in cold air is the part most people miss. Water evaporating from the skin takes warmth and moisture with it, so a child who runs outside at break with damp hands loses more than one who dries them first.

## What makes it worse

A few ordinary things keep the cycle going:

- Hot water. It feels better on cold hands but strips more of the skin's natural oils.
- Harsh foaming soaps, particularly the ones that leave hands feeling squeaky.
- Skipping moisturiser because hands are going to get washed again anyway.
- Picking at the cracked bits, which nearly every child does.

None of these cause the problem on their own. Together they stop the skin catching up.

## A routine that works

The aim is not to moisturise more. It is to lose less moisture in the first place.

Switch to lukewarm water and a gentler soap. A [foaming hand soap made for children](https://inatureltd.co.uk/products/incia-natural-foaming-hand-soap-for-kids) cleans without the stripped, tight feeling that sends a child straight back to scratching.

Dry hands properly, including between the fingers. This takes ten seconds and makes more difference than anything else on this list.

Moisturise while the hands are still slightly damp, not bone dry. There is more moisture in the skin at that moment and a cream applied then holds it there.

## What to expect

Skin that has been dry all winter does not recover in a day. With a gentler soap, proper drying and moisturiser twice a day, most children are noticeably more comfortable within a week or two.

If the skin stays sore, weeps, or the cracks look infected, that is the point to stop managing it at home and speak to your GP or pharmacist.

## Questions parents ask

**Can I use my own hand cream on my child?**
Usually yes, though adult creams are often more heavily fragranced. For skin that is already irritated, a fragrance-free or lightly scented option is kinder.

**How often should they moisturise?**
Morning and bedtime as a baseline, plus after any long hand wash. Bedtime matters most, because the cream has all night to work.

**Does hand sanitiser make it worse?**
It can. Alcohol gels dry the skin more than soap and water. When both are available, washing is the gentler choice.

## Before spring

Dry winter hands are mostly a moisture problem, not a cleaning problem. Warm water instead of hot, a gentler soap, drying properly, and cream while the skin is still damp will get most children through to spring without cracked knuckles.
