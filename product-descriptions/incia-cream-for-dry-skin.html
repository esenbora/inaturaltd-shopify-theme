The INCIA Moisturising Cream is a rich, nourishing cream made for dry, chapped and dehydrated skin. Blending lavender oil with shea butter and other natural plant oils, it comforts skin that feels tight, rough or flaky and helps it feel calmer and better looked after. It is 100% natural and dermatologically tested, with no parabens, paraffin, silicones or alcohol.

Because it skips paraffin and alcohol, this cream supports the skin's natural flora and barrier rather than smothering or drying it out. Shea butter sinks in deeply to nourish and soften, while carefully chosen oils help skin hold onto moisture. It is a lovely choice for hands, elbows, heels, knees and face, and a comforting everyday cream through cold, dry British winters.

How to use

This is a rich, quite oily cream, so a little goes a long way. Take a pea-sized amount and gently massage it into dry or rough areas until it is absorbed. Use it daily, or several times a day on very dry patches, after showering or before heading out into cold air. For a persistent skin concern, check with your GP or pharmacist.

Frequently asked questions

Can I use it on babies and children?
Yes. This cream is free from fragrance and synthetic ingredients, so it suits delicate young skin. Apply a thin layer and massage in gently: it absorbs quickly and feels soft. If in doubt, check with your GP or pharmacist first.

Is it suitable for the face?
Yes. It can be used on both face and body. The texture is rich and nourishing, which makes it especially comforting for dry or sensitive facial skin. If your skin is on the oilier side, use only a small amount.

How often can I use it?
You can use it daily, and even several times a day on very dry areas. The rich formula absorbs well and keeps skin feeling nourished. For stubborn dryness, applying it consistently works best.

Can it help with flaky, dry winter skin?
Yes. It is made to soften rough, chapped or flaky-feeling skin, and its natural oils help skin feel more comfortable while protecting against cold, dry air. For an ongoing skin concern, speak to your GP or pharmacist.
